# Curriculum Judgement: Get well soon

## Summary
The curriculum interleaves two distinct source videos (health hacks and macronutrients) into a four-lesson program, alternating between them. While individual lessons are coherent and largely faithful to the source, the program title and description are vague/empty, and the interleaving structure splits each video's content across two non-adjacent lessons, which creates a disjointed learning arc. Source faithfulness is strong with only minor concerns.

## Scores

### Lesson Boundaries: 3/5
Within each video, splits are placed at reasonable points (Lesson 1 cuts at 112s — right at the "Walking After Meals & Glass Water Bottles" segment end at 114s; Lesson 3 picks up from 112s). However, the interleaving of video A → video B → video A → video B forces unrelated topics to sit adjacent to each other, which works against natural topic continuity.

### Lesson Count Appropriateness: 4/5
Four lessons for two ~4-minute videos covering ~14 distinct ideas is reasonable. Each video gets two lessons, which respects the density. One could argue the health-hacks video deserves three lessons given it covers 10 hacks, but four is defensible.

### Title Clarity: 4/5
Titles are specific and action-oriented — e.g., "Stop demonizing carbs — understand what your body actually runs on" and "Protect your eyes, immune system, and sleep with three more daily hacks" tell the learner exactly what's inside. The program title "Get well soon" is weak and generic, and the program description is empty.

### Step Logical Flow: 3/5
Each lesson has only two steps (one DO, one REFLECT), with no WATCH step explicitly typed despite clips being attached. The DO→REFLECT pattern is consistent but thin — there's no concept-introduction step, so the flow feels truncated. Lesson 1 also drops the glass-water-bottle hack from its DO step despite the clip covering it.

### Source Faithfulness: 5/5
Every specific claim traces back to the source: "credit card worth of plastic each week," "20-20-20 rule," "Americans eat twice the protein they need and half the fiber," fat-soluble vitamins A/D/E/K, bio-individuality, and the four factors (physical activity, genetics, access to food, personal health goals) are all in the transcripts. No hallucinated examples or names detected.

### Topic Boundary Respect: 4/5
Clip-to-topic mapping is clean within each source video. For the health hacks video, both source `topics` and `segments` are respected: clip 0–112s covers the "Walking/Glass Bottles" segment (37–114s) plus intro; clip 112–225s covers "Eye Health & Cold Showers" (114–146s), "Morning Sunlight & Vinegar" (146–216s), and "Oil Pulling/Sleep/Portion" + "Legs Up the Wall" (216–end). However, Lesson 3's title only advertises three hacks (20-20-20, cold showers, morning light) while the clip 112–225s actually contains *seven* hacks including vinegar, oil pulling, sleep, portion control, and legs-up-the-wall — a significant under-promise that suggests the lesson framing doesn't fully respect what's in the clip. For the macros video, clip splits at 113s align cleanly between "Carbohydrates" (35–143s) and "Proteins" (143–217s) segments, though 113s cuts slightly before the carbs segment ends at 143s.

## Recommendations
- Fix the program title and fill in the program description — "Get well soon" with an empty description is unacceptable for a shipped curriculum.
- Lesson 3's title and summary must account for *all* hacks in the 112–225s clip (vinegar, oil pulling, sleep, portion control, legs-up-the-wall) — currently it promises three hacks but delivers seven.
- Add an explicit WATCH step type to each session so the learner knows when to consume the clip vs. when to act/reflect.
- Reconsider the interleaving structure — completing the health-hacks video before pivoting to macronutrients would create a cleaner mental model than alternating.
- Adjust the Lesson 2 → Lesson 4 clip split from 113s to 143s to align with the actual carbohydrates segment boundary in the source.
